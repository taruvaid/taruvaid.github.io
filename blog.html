01 Neural Networks

Weight Initialization Schemes in Neural Network Training

How a network's weights are set before training has an outsized effect on whether it trains at all. Initializing weights too small causes activations and gradients to shrink toward zero as they pass through layers, while initializing them too large causes them to explode — both make deep networks hard or impossible to optimize. Schemes like Xavier (Glorot) and He initialization address this by scaling the initial weights based on the number of input and output connections at each layer, keeping the variance of activations roughly constant from layer to layer. The right choice depends on the activation function in use — Xavier pairs well with sigmoid or tanh, while He initialization is designed for ReLU-family activations — and getting this right is often the difference between a network that trains smoothly and one that never leaves a poor starting point.

Key paper
Delving Deep into Rectifiers: Surpassing Human-Level Performance on ImageNet Classification ↗

He et al. (2015) introduce the Parametric ReLU (PReLU), an activation that lets the negative-side slope be learned rather than fixed at zero, and pair it with an initialization scheme built specifically for ReLU-style nonlinearities — now widely known as He initialization. Earlier schemes like Xavier assumed activations that behave symmetrically around zero, an assumption ReLUs break; this method corrects for that by scaling weight variance to account for the roughly half of activations a ReLU zeroes out. With this fix, the authors were able to train much deeper rectifier networks from scratch and reported the first result to exceed human-level accuracy on the ImageNet classification benchmark.
